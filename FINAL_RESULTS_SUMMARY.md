# Final results summary

## Research question

How does the strategy used to turn a parent's predictions into synthetic
training targets affect predictive degradation and induction-circuit measures
across recursive generations? In particular, do prediction, attention-based
induction measures, single-head ablation effects and generated-symbol behaviour
change together or on different schedules?

## Methodology

The project uses the two-layer attention-only transformer and Omniglot features
from Singh et al. (2024). Each successor is freshly initialised and trained for
1,000,000 examples on data generated by its parent; only the data, not model
parameters, pass between generations. The selected learning rate is 0.001.

The base task predicts the query label. The extended task predicts the query
label, chooses one of the two context symbols, and predicts that symbol's label.
Development used a fixed 1,000-question evaluator from 100 held-out classes.
After all reported conditions were frozen, final saved checkpoints were scored
once on a disjoint 1,000-question, 100-class reserved test. Attention and
performance were analysed at selected development checkpoints; single-head
ablations were performed only at final checkpoints. No attention or ablation
analysis was run on the final test.

## Report-worthy conditions

- Base recursive chain, generations 0–4.
- Extended `label_argmax`, generations 0–4.
- Extended `label_sampling_temperature_3`, generations 0–4.
- Extended `label_sampling_temperature_5`, generations 0–4.
- Extended `context_feedback_temperature_one_third`, generations 0–6.
- Extended `symbol_sampling_temperature_0.2`, generations 0–6.

Generation 0 is shared by the extended conditions. These are single chains, not
independent replications.

## Interpretability results

- Canonical previous-token attention averages chance-corrected predecessor
  attention at positions 1 and 3. Positions 1/3/5 are retained only as a named
  historical diagnostic.
- Changing to the canonical definition changed the selected previous-token head
  in three checked generations: label-sampling T=3 g2 (H4→H0) and next-symbol
  T=0.2 g2 (H4→H2) and g3 (H5→H7). No selected final-generation head changed.
- The corresponding earlier-generation ablation effects changed, but the final
  interpretation did not. The next-symbol T=0.2 g6 previous-token-head ablation
  remained H3 with a 12.7-point query-accuracy effect.
- Label sampling T=3 and T=5 ended with near-zero/low induction scores and poor
  label prediction. Context feedback T=1/3 ended with low induction (0.129) and
  poor prediction despite a previous-token score near 1. Next-symbol T=0.2 kept
  high query accuracy while symbol loss, following-label performance and both
  attention measures worsened. These patterns show that the measured quantities
  need not degrade together.
- Attention patterns alone are not causal evidence. The final-checkpoint
  single-head ablations do not establish that a circuit disappeared or that a
  head is necessary or redundant.

## Modified extended-task rationale and approval record

The repository-supported rationale is that restricting the next-symbol output to
the two symbols already in context creates a controlled two-way decision, reuses
the existing symbol features and adds only a two-way linear symbol head while
leaving the original backbone and label head in place. This allows recursive data
generation to vary symbol choice as well as label targets.

No local assignment brief, approval note or other artifact records lecturer
approval of this modification. The rationale above is supported by the README,
HANDOVER and implementation; it should not be described as “lecturer-approved”
in the submission unless the external approval evidence is supplied or cited.

## Supported claims

- Base recursion and extended label argmax remain stable over the reported chain.
- High-temperature label sampling causes strong predictive degradation and heavy
  corruption of generated label targets in these chains.
- Context feedback T=1/3 eventually degrades strongly despite zero recorded
  generated query-label errors.
- Next-symbol T=0.2 strongly degrades symbol loss and induction-related measures
  while query accuracy remains high; following-label accuracy is less stable.
- Predictive performance, attention-based measures and symbol behaviour do not
  necessarily degrade on the same schedule.
- The frozen final test supports the same qualitative conclusions as development.

## Limitations and claims not supported

One chain and one initialisation seed were used per condition; the 1,000-question
evaluators are samples; both learning-rate winners lie at the upper edge of their
tested grids; attention was measured at only 12 extended (13 base) checkpoints;
and ablations are single-head, final-checkpoint interventions. Context-feedback
models were evaluated on the original question distribution, not their shifted
training distribution.

Do not claim statistical generalisation across seeds or chains, global
learning-rate optimality, that attention changes preceded accuracy changes, that
an induction circuit disappeared, that context concentration caused collapse,
that both label outputs stayed intact under next-symbol T=0.2, or that the final
test was used for tuning. Do not report historical positions-1/3/5 scores as the
canonical previous-token metric or present lecturer approval as documented fact.
